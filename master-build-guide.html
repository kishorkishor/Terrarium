01 Read these five rules first

Break one of these and something dies 1 · Sensors run on 3.3V, never 5V. A soil sensor on 5V feeds 5V into an ADC pin and kills it.
2 · One common ground. Every GND — sensors, both boards, buck, adapter — ends up connected. No exceptions.
3 · Measure 5.00V at the buck output with the multimeter before wire C9 exists. This is the step that saves the ESP32.
4 · Electrolytic capacitors are polarised — stripe (−) faces GND. Backwards = pop.
5 · Board A runs at 5V, Board B at 12V. They look identical. Label them with tape the moment they arrive — swapping them puts 12V into the mist drivers.
Two habits that prevent most first-build damage Unplug power before changing any wire — every time, no exceptions. And never run a mist disc dry: water in the cup first, then power. The disc burns out in minutes without water.

03 Build it in six stages

Each stage ends with a test that proves it worked. Never move on with a failing test — a fault found in stage 2 costs minutes; the same fault found in stage 6 costs hardware.

Your multimeter — the only two modes you need Continuity ♪ (the beep symbol): beeps when the probes touch a connected path. Use only with power OFF and USB unplugged — never on a live circuit. DC Volts ⎓ (20V range on the UT33B+): black probe sits on GND, red probe touches the test point. Safe on a live circuit — that's its job. Every stage below ends with exact probe placements and the number you should see.

0Before any wiring — flash and smoke-test the bare board

  • Install the firmware toolchain following firmware/terrarium/README.md (Arduino IDE → esp32 boards → Adafruit BME280 + BH1750 libraries).
  • Plug the bare ESP32 into USB with a data cable. Upload the firmware with your Wi-Fi name/password set in config.h. Serial Monitor at 115200 prints an IP → open it on your phone. You now have the app running with no wiring at all — sensors just read "--".
  • Do the same once for the spare board, then put it back in its bag.
  • No multimeter? Get one now — stages 3 and 4 are impossible without it.
  • 🔎 METER (DC V, USB plugged): red probe on the pin printed 3V3, black on GND → 3.25–3.35V. That's the board's own regulator working. Anything under 3.1V with nothing connected = bad board — swap for the spare, claim warranty.

1Sensors on a breadboard — group A wires, USB power only

  • Push the ESP32 into a breadboard; use the breadboard's red/blue strips as the 3.3V and GND rails from the diagram.
  • Wire group A1–A19 with jumpers (M-F to the sensor modules). Tick each row below as you go.
  • Test: the phone app shows live temperature, humidity, lux, and both soil percentages. Squeeze a soil sensor's paddle in your fist — its number moves. Lift the float — the tank warning clears. If a sensor reads "--", its wires are on the wrong pins; nothing is damaged, fix and retry.
  • Calibrate here: write down each soil sensor's raw value dry and dipped in water (Serial Monitor shows them), and put those numbers into config.h. Do it per sensor — two sensors from one batch differ.
  • 🔎 METER (continuity, USB UNPLUGGED, before first power): ① ESP 3V3 pin ↔ 3.3V rail → beep. ② ESP GND ↔ GND rail → beep. ③ 3.3V rail ↔ GND rail → must NOT beep — a beep here is a short; find it before plugging anything in. This 10-second trio catches the classic first-build mistake for free.
  • 🔎 METER (DC V, USB plugged): ④ across any sensor's VCC↔GND → 3.3V. ⑤ SOIL AOUT ↔ GND → roughly 2.2–3.0V in dry air, falling to 1.0–1.5V dipped in water. If it sits pinned near 3.3V and never moves, the AOUT wire is on the wrong pin. ⑥ Float switch (continuity, on its two wires, sensor unplugged from ESP): beeps in one float position, silent in the other — write down which position beeps; that's your empty/full orientation for config.

2Control signals — group B, still USB power

  • Label the boards with tape first: "A-5V" and "B-12V". They look identical. Rule 5.
  • Wire B1–B7, and solder a 10kΩ from every IN terminal to GND (B8 — 5 places).
  • Test: app → MANUAL mode → toggle each output. Multimeter in continuity/diode mode across each board's OUT terminals clicks between open and closed as you toggle. No load power is connected yet — you're proving the signals land on the right channels.
  • 🔎 METER (DC V, USB plugged, app in MANUAL): black probe on GND. Red probe on each board IN terminal in turn — toggle that output in the app: ON = 3.3V · OFF = 0.0V. Test all five: A·IN1, A·IN2, B·IN1, B·IN2, B·IN3. A channel stuck at 0V when ON = wrong GPIO wire. Floating around 1–2V when OFF = its 10k pulldown is missing (B8).

3Power — group C, the careful stage

  • Build the chain in order: barrel jack → fuse holder (3A fitted) → rocker → 12V bus (C1–C4).
  • Feed the buck (C5–C6). Now C8: plug in, switch on, measure the buck's OUT terminals. It must read 5.0V ±0.1 with NOTHING connected to it. Wrong voltage → stop, nothing is damaged yet.
  • Unplug. Only now wire C9–C14 (5V to ESP32 + Board A · 12V to Board B) and fit the three capacitors C15–C17, stripes to GND.
  • Test: unplug USB entirely. Power from the wall. The ESP32 boots, the app loads, sensors read. Your build is now mains-powered and self-contained.
  • 🔎 METER (continuity, BEFORE the first wall plug-in): 12V bus ↔ GND bus → no beep. 5V rail ↔ GND bar → no beep. A beep = short circuit; the fuse would blow the moment you plug in. Fix first.
  • 🔎 METER (DC V, powered, black probe on GND bus) — walk the chain left to right: barrel + lead ≈12V → fuse output 12V (0V here = blown or badly seated fuse) → rocker output 12V with the lamp lit → buck IN+ 12V → C8: buck OUT+ = 4.9–5.1V, nothing connected to it. After C9–C17: ESP VIN 5V, ESP 3V3 3.3V, Board A DC+ 5V, Board B DC+ 12V. If Board A reads 12V — STOP, the boards are swapped (Rule 5); the mist drivers survive only because nothing is connected yet.

4Loads — group D, water in the cups first

  • Wire the mist units (D1–D4), light (D5–D6), fans + their diodes (D7–D12). Screw terminals — no bare twisted joints.
  • Mist test: discs sitting in water, MANUAL mode, toggle Watering — mist rises instantly (these kits auto-start; the built-in 4-hour timer is irrelevant because the board power-cycles them every burst). Toggle Humidity — same.
  • Interlock test: lift the float switch out of the water — both mist channels must die instantly and refuse to switch on even in MANUAL. Put it back — control returns. This is your proposal's safety demo.
  • Fans spin, light lights, brightness slider dims it. Done — the machine works.
  • 🔎 METER (DC V, probes ACROSS each load's two terminals, app in MANUAL): output OFF → ≈0V across the load. Output ON → full supply across it: mist units ≈5V, light and fans ≈12V. A load showing half-voltage or running weak = wrong board voltage or a missing ground. A fan that twitches but won't spin = check its diode isn't backwards (stripe to +).

5Make it permanent — veroboard

  • Re-build the breadboard layout on veroboard: two rows of 19 female headers as a socket (the ESP32 stays removable), copper strips as the 3.3V/GND rails, screw terminals for every sensor and load cable.
  • Heat-shrink every wire-to-wire joint. Hot glue as strain relief where cables leave the board.
  • Re-run the stage 1–4 tests after the move. Solder bridges hide here — find them now.
  • Mount the finished board in a dry box above the water line, cables entering from below.
  • 🔎 METER (continuity, unpowered, after every soldering session): re-run the stage-1 trio (3V3↔rail beep · GND↔rail beep · 3V3↔GND no beep), then probe every pair of neighbouring copper strips around your new joints — any beep between adjacent strips is a solder bridge. Add C19 (rail → GND bus bond). Then re-run the stage-3 voltage walk before trusting it.

648-hour soak, then plants

  • Run the empty tank two days on AUTO. Watch for: condensation where it shouldn't be, tubing creep, humidity stuck at 100% (move the BME280 higher, out of the mist plume), anything warm to the touch.
  • Trigger the empty-tank state once a day on purpose. It must always win.
  • Then substrate, plants, and the grow light's final position. Calibrate thresholds from the app over the first week.
