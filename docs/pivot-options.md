## 0. The decision in one page

You have: ESP32 + BME280 + BH1750, ultrasonic mist makers, a fan, an LED strip, a pump, humidity hysteresis with bounded bursts, offline-first firmware, and a planned slide damper. What none of the terrarium papers have is a **controlled comparison on a fast-growing organism**. So pick an organism whose whole life cycle is shorter than a month and whose growth depends on exactly the variables your box controls.

**Why A.** Oyster mushrooms want 85 to 95 % RH, 20 to 28 °C, low light, and fresh air, and they punish bad ventilation visibly: high CO2 gives long stems and small caps. That makes your slide damper a real actuator with a measurable outcome. Pinning starts about 5 to 7 days after the block is opened and harvest follows about 5 days later, so a whole flush fits in two weeks. Spawn and ready fruiting blocks are cheap and available in Bangladesh (the National Mushroom Development Institute at Savar and private growers sell bags).

**The paper A would be:** "A low-cost fruiting chamber with humidity hysteresis and CO2-actuated fresh-air exchange: controller comparison on *Pleurotus ostreatus*." Contribution: (1) damper plus CO2 feedback as a ventilation actuator at chamber scale, (2) side-by-side comparison of timer, threshold and hysteresis-plus-CO2 control on yield, biological efficiency, cap-to-stem ratio, water and energy use, (3) fault handling (tank empty, sensor loss, power cut) which no mushroom paper reports.

## 1. Design changes for option A

1. **Twin bays.** Turn the three-segment box into two identical growing bays sharing the electronics segment, each with its own damper, mist line and sensors. That lets you run treatment and baseline at the same time on blocks from the same batch, which removes the biggest confound (block-to-block variation). Two bays of about 30 x 30 x 40 cm each hold 2 to 3 blocks.
2. **CO2 sensor.** One NDIR sensor per bay (SCD40 or MH-Z19C, about 15 to 25 USD each). Everything else in the mushroom literature uses DHT11/22 only.
3. **Servo on the slide damper.** A 9 g servo on the finger tab. Damper opens when CO2 exceeds a threshold (start at 1000 ppm, close at 800 ppm) or RH is above the upper band.
4. **Reservoir on a load cell** (HX711 + 5 kg cell) to log water use. Only one paper (Foffano 2025) reports water per kg of mushrooms.
5. **On-device logging** to SD or flash, 1-minute samples: T, RH, CO2, lux, damper state, mist and fan duty, water mass.
6. **Optional:** ESP32-CAM per bay for time-lapse; use it for harvest timing and cap measurements.
7. **Peltier is optional.** Dhaka room temperature in most months is inside the 20 to 28 °C fruiting window. If you add it, the cold side belongs in the air unit with a drip tray, as discussed.

## 2. Experiment plan for option A

- **Arms:** (T) timer misting 30 s every 30 min plus fan 5 min per hour; (H) your hysteresis RH control with fan on a fixed schedule; (HC) hysteresis RH plus CO2-actuated damper. Run two arms at a time in the twin bays, swap bays on the second round to cancel bay effects.
- **Blocks:** 2 to 3 per bay per round, all from the same spawn batch, weighed at start.
- **Rounds:** round 1 (T vs HC), round 2 (H vs HC), round 3 repeats with bays swapped. About 6 to 8 weeks total; first publishable data after 4 weeks.
- **Outcomes:** fresh yield per block, biological efficiency (yield / dry substrate mass), days to pinning, days to harvest, cap diameter / stem length, water used per kg, mister and fan energy (duty x rated power), time-in-band for RH and CO2, number of actuator switches.
- **Fault table:** tank empty, RH sensor unplugged, CO2 sensor unplugged, Wi-Fi off, mains cut and restore. Log what the controller did and how long each bay stayed out of band.
- **Statistics:** paired comparison per round (Welch t or Mann-Whitney on per-block yield), report effect sizes and 95 % CI. Rukhiran 2023 used a plain t-test; do better by reporting both.

## 3. Papers for option A (oyster mushroom chambers)

Each entry: what they did, main finding, gap versus your plan.

1. **Chong et al. 2023, Biosensors, "IoT-based environmental monitoring and control system for home-based mushroom cultivation."** <https://doi.org/10.3390/bios13010098> NodeMCU, DHT sensors, cloud server, mobile and web app, live video of the chamber. Sensors checked against manual readings; the control loop switches equipment from sensor feedback. Most-cited paper in the niche (over 100 citations). Gap: threshold control, cloud dependent, no CO2 sensor, no yield or growth outcome reported.
2. **Rukhiran et al. 2023, Sustainability, "IoT-based mushroom cultivation system with solar renewable energy integration."** <https://doi.org/10.3390/su151813968> Compared IoT-controlled vs conventional cultivation. Average yield 5.306 kg vs 4.118 kg, t-test p close to 0. Gap: compares "automated" against "manual", not one controller against another; no CO2; no water or energy per kg. This is the yield-comparison precedent you cite and improve on.
3. **Foffano et al. 2025, Sustainability, "Design and evaluation of a compact IoT-enabled microfarm ... Pleurotus ostreatus."** <https://doi.org/10.3390/su172210332> Retrofitted glass-door fridge with custom microcontroller. Held 25.0 +/- 0.7 °C and 90 +/- 8 % RH; 230 g per block, biological efficiency 44 %, water footprint 4.39 L per kg. Gap: +/- 8 % RH swing (your hysteresis band should beat it), no ventilation actuator or CO2 feedback described, single controller, no fault handling. Closest neighbour: benchmark your numbers against theirs.
4. **Lagrimas and Timajo 2026, TechMecha RoboSphere, "Development of fogponics technology for mushroom production."** <https://doi.org/10.62718/vmca.tech-robo.1.1.sc-0126-006> Ultrasonic fog chamber, two fan speeds, 120 bags, 60 days. 3000 rpm gave 11.3 kg and 59.8 % BE vs 9.5 kg and 50.1 % at 1500 rpm, p < 0.001. Gap: the authors themselves call for multiple airflow levels and direct airflow measurement. No CO2 measured, so they cannot say why airflow helped. Your CO2-actuated damper answers exactly this. Note: new and little-known journal, cite it for the finding but do not lean on it.
5. **Elewi et al. 2024, Smart Agricultural Technology, "Design and implementation of a cost-aware and smart oyster mushroom cultivation system."** <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.atech.2024.100439> Low-cost system for displaced communities in northern Syria; monitors temperature, humidity, air quality and light with web dashboards. Gap: engineering description, no controlled yield experiment, no controller comparison. Good venue signal: Smart Agricultural Technology publishes this kind of work.
6. **Ten et al. 2021, Advances in Agricultural and Food Research J., "Automated controlled environment mushroom house."** <https://doi.org/10.36877/aafrj.a0000230> Greenhouse converted to an IoT mushroom house for grey oyster; 18 to 27 °C, RH at least 70 %; reports at least 30 % more yield and contamination under 2 %. Gap: greenhouse scale, control algorithm not described, no CO2.
7. **Wajiran et al. 2020, J. Phys. Conf. Ser., "Control and realtime monitoring system for mushroom cultivation fields based on WSN and IoT."** <https://doi.org/10.1088/1742-6596/1655/1/012003> Arduino + XBee nodes, Raspberry Pi master, Firebase. Keeps 26 to 29 °C and 70 to 90 % RH; sensor errors 0.47 °C and 2.7 % RH. Gap: functionality test only, no crop outcome.
8. **Setiawati et al. 2021, IOP Conf. Ser. Earth Environ. Sci., "Design of temperature and humidity control system on oyster mushroom plant house based on IoT."** <https://doi.org/10.1088/1755-1315/712/1/012002> DHT11 calibrated against a hygrometer (R² 0.86 for RH), setpoints 28 °C and 85 %, Blynk app. Treated mushrooms were white and soft, untreated yellowish and dry. Gap: qualitative outcome, DHT11 (poor RH accuracy above 80 %), no CO2.
9. **Wibowo et al. 2023, PROtek, "Implementation of monitoring and control temperature and humidity based on IoT in the oyster mushroom cultivation room."** <https://doi.org/10.33387/protk.v10i2.4863> Reports recovery times: 383 s of heating to return to 25 to 28 °C, 115 s of watering to return to 75 to 90 % RH. Gap: no yield, no comparison; but recovery time is a metric you can also report.
10. **Setiawan et al. 2021, JMPM, "Design and manufacture an automatic mushroom sprinkler based IoT to increase oyster mushroom productivity."** <https://doi.org/10.18196/jmpm.v5i1.12043> Piezo mister with blower, DHT11, ATmega16; claims 34.5 % productivity increase over manual watering. Gap: no control-strategy comparison, no CO2.
11. **Suresh et al. 2021, IOP Conf. Ser. Mater. Sci. Eng., "Monitoring and automatic control of various parameters for mushroom farming."** <https://doi.org/10.1088/1757-899x/1055/1/012011> Proposes a Peltier air-conditioner with honeycomb pad because earlier systems could not reach the needed cooling. Gap: proposal-level, no measured results. Relevant if you add the Peltier.
12. **Bautista et al. 2023, IEEE HNICEM, "Humidity and temperature control system for oyster mushroom."** <https://doi.org/10.1109/hnicem60674.2023.10589133> ATmega328P + ESP32 + DHT22; pump and fan switch on thresholds; errors 2.6 % (T) and 5.1 % (RH). Gap: functional test only.
13. **Elenzano 2023, IEEE ICTBIG, "IoT-based smart environmental monitoring for oyster mushroom production."** <https://doi.org/10.1109/ictbig59752.2023.10456207> Arduino misting triggered above 30 °C, RTC and SD-card logging. Gap: misting used for cooling only, no RH control loop, no outcome.
14. **Moysiadis et al. 2023, Agriculture, "Monitoring mushroom growth with machine learning."** <https://doi.org/10.3390/agriculture13010223> YOLOv5 detects Pleurotus growth stage, F1 up to 76.5 %; average growth period 5.22 days; harvest-day prediction. Use: justifies an ESP32-CAM for harvest timing; you do not need to redo the ML.
15. **Chen et al. 2022, Int. J. Agric. Biol. Eng., "Research progress on indoor environment of mushroom factory" (review).** <https://doi.org/10.25165/j.ijabe.20221501.6872> Reviews monitoring systems, CFD airflow modelling and facility design; names missing design guidelines and energy-saving strategies as open problems. Use: the review that frames ventilation as the unsolved part.

## 4. Control-method precedents to cite (any option)

16. **García-Mañas et al. 2024, European J. Control, "A practical solution for multivariable control of temperature and humidity in greenhouses."** <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejcon.2024.100967> Ventilation plus fogging plus dehumidifier controlled with PI and inverted decoupling; split-range control of humidity. Integral absolute error reduced by about 88 % vs decentralized PI. Use: the precedent for treating ventilation and fog as coupled actuators; you do it at chamber scale with cheap parts.
17. **Cevallos et al. 2022, Agriculture, "A practical hybrid control approach for a greenhouse microclimate: a hardware-in-the-loop implementation."** <https://doi.org/10.3390/agriculture12111916> On-off, PID and fuzzy-PI compared for vapour-pressure-deficit control in HIL simulation. Use: shows controller comparison is publishable; yours is on real biology, not a simulator.
18. **Garrido López et al. 2025, Sensors, "Reproducing cold-chain conditions in real time using a controlled Peltier-based climate system."** <https://doi.org/10.3390/s25216689> Peltier chamber with cascaded PID, 36-day validation, plus a 24-hour comparison against baseline controllers; MAE 0.19 °C. Use: methodological template (long run plus short baseline comparison) and the Peltier reference if you add cooling.
19. **Vernon et al. 2025, Sensors, "Microclimate-controlled smart growth cabinets for high-throughput plant phenotyping."** <https://doi.org/10.3390/s25247509> Programmable multi-cabinet system under 10 000 AUD; +/- 2 °C for 97 % of the time; automated image plus sensor datasets. Use: the "expensive end" you undercut by two orders of magnitude.

## 5. Papers for option B (duckweed fog chamber)

20. **Loaiza and Jansen 2026, Int. J. Phytoremediation, "Fog-o-ponic cultivation of duckweed (Lemna minor L.)."** <https://doi.org/10.1080/15226514.2026.2619609> Lemna grown on fabric fed by nutrient fog instead of a water column. Relative growth rate 0.24 per day, Fv/Fm about 0.8, nitrogen removal about 500 mg per m² per day, matching liquid culture. Gap: manual fog schedule, no automation, no closed-loop humidity, no image-based growth measurement, zero citations so far. Your mist maker plus ESP32 is the obvious next paper.
21. **Jung, Nandi and McDonald 2025, Applied Sciences, "SIPEREA: a scalable imaging platform for measuring two-dimensional growth of duckweed."** <https://doi.org/10.3390/app16010066> ESP32-CAM modules, wireless image collection, DNN segmentation, doubling time computed over two weeks. Gap: measurement only, no environmental control. Combine with 20 and you have a closed loop.
22. **Pasos-Panqueva, Baker and Camargo-Valero 2024, J. Environmental Management, "Unravelling the impact of light, temperature and nutrient dynamics on duckweed growth: a meta-analysis."** <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jenvman.2024.121721> Growth peaks at 11.4 to 32.3 °C, daily light integral 5 to 20 mol per m², N above 5 mg/L, P above 1 mg/L, N:P about 15:1. Use: gives you the setpoints; a chamber that holds DLI and temperature inside these bands is the design target.
23. **Coughlan et al. 2022, J. Cleaner Production, "Duckweed bioreactors: challenges and opportunities for large-scale indoor cultivation of Lemnaceae."** <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jclepro.2021.130285> Doubling times down to 1.24 days indoors; proposes stacked systems with 5 cm water depth; says sensor and AI-supported autonomous cropping is the missing piece. Use: the review that states the gap.
24. **Haffner et al. 2020, Water, "Lemna minor bioassay evaluation using computer image analysis."** <https://doi.org/10.3390/w12082207> ISO 20079 168-hour growth test scored by frond area instead of frond count; area was faster and more sensitive. Use: the 7-day protocol and the area metric for your runs.
25. **Redmond et al. 2025, Plants, "Varied growth media necessitate different light regimes for indoor duckweed cultivation."** <https://doi.org/10.3390/plants14030397> Photoperiod mattered more than intensity in small static trials, intensity mattered more in a flowing multi-tier system. Use: shows that small-scale results do not transfer, which is a reason a controlled chamber study is still needed.
