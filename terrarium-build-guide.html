01 Design decisions

The written proposal and the later revision specified two different machines. These are the resolutions, and why each one won.

ChoiceSourceReasoning
BME280 over DHT22revision DHT22 is ±5% RH and saturates permanently above ~90% RH — useless inside a terrarium. BME280 reads 0–100% RH and shares the I²C bus, costing zero extra GPIO versus DHT22's dedicated pin.
MOSFETs over relaysrevision Silent, no coil back-EMF, no contact wear, and PWM-capable — so the grow light dims and the fans run at partial speed. Relays give on/off only.
12V main rail over all-5Vrevision 5V pumps and USB grow lights are toy-grade. 12V buys a real peristaltic dosing pump and a real light, with a buck converter feeding the 5V logic.
Keep the OLEDproposal The revision cut it. Restored — it is the visible proof of the "continues operating without Wi-Fi" claim, demonstrable to an examiner without a phone.
Drop the DS3231 RTCrevision ESP32 NTP over Wi-Fi, with a millis() fallback schedule for offline operation.
Drop the moat/circulation pumpthis audit Appears in no stated objective, and adds a second pump plus tubing near water for no graded benefit. A spare MOSFET channel (GPIO23) is reserved if it returns.
Camera is optionalthis audit Not present in any of the six project objectives. Listed separately and unticked by default.

02 The pin-count correction

Corrects a common assumption

A 38-pin ESP32 gives you almost no extra usable GPIO over a 30-pin one. The eight extra pads are GPIO6–11 (SD0–SD3, CLK, CMD), hardwired to the SPI flash chip. Drive any of them and the board fails to boot. Both variants expose roughly 19 usable pins.

And sensors are cheaper in pins than expected

A BME280 needs four wires — but only two GPIO. The BH1750 and the OLED share those same two pins on the I²C bus. Three devices, two pins. That is why this build fits comfortably inside either board.

Required14 GPIO
Available (safe)19 GPIO
Spare after build13 · 14 · 33 · 36 · 39
Never use6–11 · 0 · 2 · 5 · 12 · 15 · 1 · 3

Buy the 38-pin variant anyway — not for the pin count, but for the extra GND and 3V3 pads, which make wiring eight sensors substantially less painful.

Module variant matters

Must be WROOM-32, not WROVER. WROVER modules use GPIO16/17 internally for PSRAM — this pin map puts the irrigation pump and exhaust fan there.

05 Power architecture

Two isolated supplies. The 24V fogger keeps its own adapter so ultrasonic switching noise never reaches the logic rail.

12V rail budget

  • Peristaltic pump — 0.40A
  • Two 40mm fans — 0.20A
  • Grow light — ~1.00A
  • Buck converter draw — 0.15A
  • Worst case 1.75A on a 5A supply — 2.8× headroom

3.3V rail budget

  • OLED — 20mA · soil ×2 — 10mA
  • Leak sensor — 10mA · DS18B20 — 1.5mA
  • BME280 — 1mA · BH1750 — 0.2mA
  • ≈43mA total — well inside the onboard AMS1117
  • No separate 3.3V regulator needed

06 Compatibility checks

Every part verified against every other part. These are the ones that would actually have caused failures — red-numbered items destroy hardware or silently corrupt data.

  1. ADC2 stops working when Wi-Fi is on. GPIO 0/2/4/12–15/25–27 share silicon with the radio, and the radio wins. Every analog input in this build is on ADC1 (32 / 34 / 35). This is the most common ESP32 IoT bug and it is silent — the code compiles, the board runs, and the numbers are simply wrong.
  2. GPIO34–39 have no internal pull-up. They are input-only and unpulled, so the float switch cannot live there. It is on GPIO27, which supports INPUT_PULLUP.
  3. Power the soil sensors from 3.3V, not 5V. At 5V their analog output swings to roughly 3.0V — sitting right at the ESP32's ADC ceiling with no margin for a bad batch. At 3.3V the output physically cannot exceed the limit.
  4. Power the leak sensor from 3.3V, not 5V. The listing states 5–18V, but the module is interdigitated traces plus a resistor — its output tracks Vcc when wet. At 5V it puts 5V directly into GPIO32 and destroys that pin. At 3.3V it detects water perfectly well, and wet-versus-dry is all an alarm needs.
  5. I²C addresses do not collide. BME280 0x76 · BH1750 0x23 · OLED 0x3C. Three devices, two pins, zero conflicts. Each module carries its own pull-ups; three in parallel gives roughly 1.6kΩ, which the ESP32 drives comfortably.
  6. 3.3V current budget clears. Total sensor draw ≈43mA against an AMS1117 with substantial headroom. No auxiliary regulator required.
  7. 12V current budget clears. 1.75A worst case against a 5A adapter.
  8. Boot glitch — the failure that floods the terrarium. Every GPIO is high-impedance during reset, and a floating MOSFET input can read HIGH, running the pump while the board boots. Fit a 10kΩ resistor from each of GPIO 16/17/18/19/23/25/26 to GND. About ৳20 of parts; without it, every power cycle is a coin flip.
  9. Flyback diodes are mandatory. The pump and fans are inductive, and low-side MOSFET switching means the coil kicks the drain above +12V at turn-off. Fit a 1N5819 across each motor, cathode to +12V. Neither MOSFET module includes one.
  10. Both switching modules accept 3.3V logic. D4184: 2.5–20V input, 6–36V output, 10A — covers the 12V pump and the 24V fogger alike. F5305S 4-channel: 3–20V input, 5–36V output, isolated, PWM-capable.
  11. The 24V adapter runs at 80% load. The fogger draws 0.8A from its included 1A supply. Acceptable — but hang nothing else on that rail.
  12. Ground everything at one star point. 12V GND, 24V GND and ESP32 GND meet at a single node on the veroboard. Both adapters are isolated SMPS secondaries, so this is safe and required for low-side switching.
  13. The DS18B20 needs an external 4.7kΩ pull-up from data to 3.3V. The bare probe does not include one.
  14. Float switch polarity is orientation-dependent. The "down" mount variant may read normally-open or normally-closed depending on how it is fitted. Test it before writing the interlock, then invert in software if needed.

Threshold plus hysteresis, with two interlocks that cannot be manually bypassed.

Offline behaviour

Automation lives on the board, not in the cloud. If Wi-Fi drops, the ESP32 continues the full control loop and shows connection status on the OLED. NTP sets the clock when a network is present; a millis() schedule carries the photoperiod when it is not.

Design caution worth writing into your evaluation section

Every RH sensor, BME280 included, drifts in permanently condensing air. Mount it high in the air space, away from the glass and outside the mist plume — otherwise it reads 100% forever and the misting logic never releases.

The sealant trap

Buy 100% silicone with no anti-mould or fungicide additive. Standard bathroom and kitchen sealant is sold as "mould resistant" precisely because it contains biocides — which leach out in a warm sealed enclosure and kill your plants and moss. Check the tube says aquarium-safe, or that the ingredients list nothing beyond silicone. Cure it fully with the lid off for 48 hours before planting.

10 Simulate & learn before you wire

Your proposal names Wokwi, and that is the right choice — but it answers only half of what you want. Wokwi proves your firmware and pin map are correct. It cannot tell you whether a connection will destroy something, because it does not simulate electricity at all. Those are two different problems and they need two different tools.

Understand this before you rely on it

Wokwi is a functional simulator, not an electrical one. Connect 12V to a 3.3V pin in Wokwi and it will happily keep running. It models no current, no voltage limits, no reverse polarity, no magic smoke. Nothing in this section replaces the multimeter checks in §11 — simulation catches logic mistakes, and the bring-up order catches electrical ones.

Which tool for which question

ToolCostAnswersCannot answer
Wokwi Browser · no install · real ESP32 emulation Free Is my pin map right? Does the hysteresis work? Do the interlocks actually block the pump? Do three I²C devices coexist? Does the sketch compile and run? Anything electrical. Also has no BME280 or BH1750 part — see substitutions below.
Falstad CircuitJS Browser · real analog solver Free Will this burn? Low-side MOSFET switching, the flyback diode clamping the pump's back-EMF, the 10kΩ gate pulldown holding the gate low, leak-sensor divider maths. No microcontroller. You model the power section by hand, not the firmware.
Tinkercad Circuits Browser · breadboard view Free Practising the MOSFET + motor + flyback subcircuit on a virtual breadboard before doing it for real. Genuinely good for building the muscle memory. No ESP32 — Arduino only. Useless for your pin map.
Proteus 8 Desktop · check whether your department has a licence Paid Formal schematic plus mixed-signal simulation in one document. Common in AIUB-style coursework, so it may be what your supervisor expects to see. No Wi-Fi simulation. ESP32 needs a third-party library added manually.
Recommended path

Start in Wokwi — it costs nothing, needs no install, and it is what your proposal already commits to. Use Falstad for one afternoon on the switching section alone. Only reach for Proteus if your department requires it as a deliverable.

Test cases to run in the simulator

Your proposal lists these — drive each one with the potentiometers and the slide switch, and confirm the correct output LED responds:

Normal operation

  • Dry soil → pump LED for 2–5s, then soak delay before re-checking
  • Low humidity → mist LED, capped by max runtime
  • High temperature → exhaust fan LED
  • Low light during the day window → grow-light LED

The failure cases that matter more

  • Float switch flipped → pump and mist must refuse to run, buzzer on
  • Leak pot raised → both water outputs off, and manual mode must not override it
  • Both faults at once → leak takes priority
  • Simulated Wi-Fi loss → automatic control continues unaffected

11 Bench bring-up order

Test in this order and a mistake costs you one component instead of the whole board. Nothing gets connected to the terrarium until step 11.

  1. Multimeter on the 12V adapter, nothing connected. Confirm voltage and centre-pin polarity. Barrel jacks are not standardised — a reverse-polarity adapter destroys everything downstream in one second.
  2. Buck converter alone, output floating. Feed it 12V, measure the output, confirm it reads 5.0V before anything is attached. If you bought the adjustable XL4015 against advice, this is the step that saves your board.
  3. ESP32 alone on USB. Upload a blink sketch. Proves the board and toolchain work before any wiring exists to blame.
  4. I²C scan, one device at a time. BME280 first — expect 0x76. Add BH1750 — 0x23. Add the OLED — 0x3C. Adding them together and getting silence tells you nothing about which one is dead.
  5. Each analog sensor separately, powered from 3.3V. Print the raw ADC value. Confirm it moves with wet/dry and never pins at the rail.
  6. Soil calibration — record the numbers. For each sensor individually: ADC reading in dry air, then fully submerged. Those two endpoints per sensor are report data, and they differ between sensors from the same batch.
  7. Float switch on the multimeter. Continuity in both orientations. Write down whether yours is normally-open or normally-closed before you code the interlock.
  8. DS18B20 with its 4.7kΩ pull-up fitted. Confirm a plausible room temperature. No pull-up means it reads -127 or 85.
  9. MOSFET modules driving an LED or a resistor — never the pump. Run wokwi/sketch.ino from §10 on the real board here: it pulses each output alone, so a swapped pair is immediately visible. Then power-cycle with the loads attached and confirm nothing pulses on during boot — this is where you verify the 10kΩ gate pulldowns from check 08 actually work.
  10. Fan, then pump — dry, into a beaker. Run ten timed cycles and record the mL each delivers. That is your irrigation-volume metric and its repeatability figure, done.
  11. Mist maker last, on its own 24V adapter, disc submerged to the depth its instructions specify, distilled water only.
  12. 48 hours running with the tank planted but unattended-safe. Watch for condensation collecting somewhere unintended, tubing creep, and whether the RH sensor ever falls back below your upper threshold. Then trigger the empty-reservoir and leak states deliberately and confirm both shut the water outputs down.
Two standing rules

Keep the electronics box above reservoir level for the whole build, not just the final assembly — a knocked-over beaker on the bench is the same failure as a leak in the tank. And do not leave the system running unattended until it has completed step 12 cleanly.
