01 The complete circuit

Click any wire to trace it. Click empty space to reset.

Positions are logical, not physical — find each ESP32 pin by its printed label. On 30-pin DevKit V1 boards GPIO16 is printed RX2; on 38-pin boards it is printed 16.

The five rules that prevent dead hardware 1 · Sensors get 3.3V, never 5V — a soil sensor on 5V feeds 5V into an ADC pin and kills it.
2 · One common ground. ESP32 GND returns to the buck's OUT− (wire C9); adapter −, buck IN−, and both MOSFET boards all tie to the GND bus.
3 · Verify 5.00V at the buck output with the multimeter before connecting anything to it (C7).
4 · Electrolytic capacitors have polarity — stripe side (−) to GND. Backwards = pop.
5 · The fuse goes on the 12V positive wire, immediately after the adapter, before every branch.

The mist-module trap — check before buying

Many cheap USB mist modules have a touch button and only start after you press it — power alone does nothing. The ESP32 can only switch power, so a button-type module will never turn on remotely. Buy the type that starts misting the instant power is applied (bare D20mm disc + driver-PCB kits usually do). Test on arrival: plug into USB — if it mists immediately with no touch, it works; if it needs a press, solder a bridge across the button's contacts or exchange it.

First power-on, in this order 1 · Group A only, ESP32 on USB — flash the firmware, open the app, see live temp/humidity/soil/lux.
2 · Add group B, still USB — app in MANUAL, toggle outputs; multimeter on each MOSFET output shows switching.
3 · Group C — buck verified at 5.00V (C7) before C8; then power the whole board from the adapter.
4 · Group D — mist discs in water first (never run them dry), then toggle each load from the phone.
